Glioma (Astrocytoma / Glioblastoma)

Intra-Axial Malignant Neoplasm • High Risk
Cellular Origin: Glial support cells (Astrocytes, Oligodendrocytes, Ependymal cells).
Epidemiology: ~80% of primary malignant brain tumors (~5.3 cases per 100,000 annually).
Hallmark Neuroimaging Presentation:

Poorly defined infiltrative margins, marked peritumoral vasogenic edema on T2/FLAIR, central necrosis with irregular thick peripheral nodular ring enhancement on post-gadolinium T1-weighted MRI.

Pathognomonic Molecular Biomarkers:
IDH1/IDH2 (R132H) 1p/19q co-deletion MGMT methylation TERT promoter EGFR amp
Frequent Clinical Manifestations:
  • Progressive morning headaches with projectile vomiting
  • New-onset adult focal motor or generalized seizures
  • Contralateral hemiparesis and sensory deficit
  • Subacute cognitive decline, executive dysfunction & personality change
Therapeutic Protocol:

Maximal safe microsurgical resection, Stupp Protocol (concomitant Radiotherapy 60 Gy + Temozolomide), 6 maintenance TMZ cycles, and Tumor Treating Fields (TTFields).

ResNet-18 Grad-CAM Saliency:

Intense focal convergence along infiltrative necrotic ring transitions and peritumoral edema boundaries on Layer 4 feature maps.

WHO Grade I (80-85%), Grade II (15%), Grade III (1-3%)

Meningioma

Extra-Axial Mesenchymal Neoplasm • Moderate Risk
Cellular Origin: Arachnoid cap cells of arachnoid villi within the meningeal dura mater.
Epidemiology: Most common primary non-glial intracranial neoplasm (~37-39% of all adult CNS tumors).
Hallmark Neuroimaging Presentation:

Sharply circumscribed extra-axial mass with broad dural base, pathognomonic "Dural Tail Sign" on contrast T1, avid homogeneous enhancement, CSF vascular cleft sign, and occasional adjacent hyperostosis.

Pathognomonic Molecular Biomarkers:
NF2 (22q loss) TRAF7 & KLF4 AKT1 & SMO CDKN2A/B deletion
Frequent Clinical Manifestations:
  • Insidious, slowly progressing dull cephalalgia
  • Focal motor seizures due to cortical compression
  • Cranial nerve deficits (anosmia, diplopia, facial numbness)
  • Visual field disturbances (sphenoid wing or tuberculum sellae)
Therapeutic Protocol:

Active observation with serial MRI for small asymptomatic lesions; complete microsurgical extirpation (Simpson Grade I-III goal); stereotactic radiosurgery for skull base lesions.

ResNet-18 Grad-CAM Saliency:

Sharp, compact spherical activation maps centered along the dural border interface and homogeneous contrast-enhanced parenchyma.

Benign Neuroendocrine (Micro & Macroadenoma)

Pituitary Tumor (PitNET / Adenoma)

Sellar / Suprasellar Neoplasm • Moderate Risk
Cellular Origin: Adenohypophyseal specialized neuroendocrine cells of the anterior pituitary gland.
Epidemiology: ~10-15% of all intracranial neoplasms; incidental autopsy prevalence up to 16.7%.
Hallmark Neuroimaging Presentation:

Expansion of sella turcica with suprasellar extension, classic "figure-of-8" or "snowman" waist at diaphragmatic aperture, superior displacement of optic chiasm, and lateral cavernous sinus extension.

Pathognomonic Molecular Biomarkers:
Pit-1 (GH, PRL, TSH) T-pit (ACTH / Cushing) SF-1 (Gonadotroph) MEN1 & AIP germline
Frequent Clinical Manifestations:
  • Bitemporal hemianopsia from midline optic chiasm compression
  • Hyperprolactinemia (galactorrhea, amenorrhea, sexual dysfunction)
  • Acromegaly / gigantism (excess Growth Hormone)
  • Cushing's disease (ACTH excess with central adiposity)
Therapeutic Protocol:

Dopamine agonist pharmacotherapy (Cabergoline, Bromocriptine) first-line for prolactinomas; Endoscopic endonasal transsphenoidal surgery (TSS) for macroadenomas.

ResNet-18 Grad-CAM Saliency:

Model activation cleanly converges onto the sellar-suprasellar midline floor, segmenting the pituitary fossa from adjacent brainstem structures.

Non-Pathological Anatomical Baseline

Healthy Brain MRI (No Tumor / Normal Control)

Normal Structural Anatomy • Low Risk
Cellular Origin: Intact neuroglial parenchymal architecture without cellular atypia or dysplasia.
Epidemiology: Negative control cohort in clinical neuroimaging screening protocols.
Hallmark Neuroimaging Presentation:

Bilateral cerebral and cerebellar hemispheric symmetry; intact midline falx cerebri with no mass effect; patent lateral, third, and fourth ventricles without dilatation; intact gray-white matter differentiation; preserved sulcal spacing.

Pathognomonic Molecular Biomarkers:
Wild-type baseline Intact BBB integrity Symmetric CMRO2 No driver mutations
Frequent Clinical Manifestations:
  • Absence of focal neurologic deficits
  • Normative intracranial pressure hemodynamics
  • Physiological cranial nerve examination
  • Intact visual acuity and peripheral visual fields
Therapeutic Protocol:

Routine reassurance; no neurosurgical or oncological intervention indicated. Diagnostic follow-up only in the event of emergent atypical neurological indicators.

ResNet-18 Grad-CAM Saliency:

Diffused, low-magnitude uniform activation throughout the cortical mantle with absence of localized high-gradient anomaly clusters.

Explainable AI: Grad-CAM Formulation

$$\alpha_k^c = \frac{1}{Z} \sum_{i=1}^u \sum_{j=1}^v \frac{\partial Y^c}{\partial A_{i,j}^k}$$

Neuron Importance Weight ($\alpha_k^c$): Global average pooling of gradients of the target class score $Y^c$ with respect to feature activation map $k$.

$$L_{\text{Grad-CAM}}^c = \text{ReLU}\left( \sum_k \alpha_k^c A^k \right)$$

Localization Map: Linear combination of forward activation maps weighted by importance, passed through ReLU to isolate features with positive influence.

Individual Project Defense & Viva Voce Guide

Comprehensive questions, answers, and technical rationale tailored for academic examiners and evaluators.

Examiner Q&A Prepared
Q1. Why did you choose ResNet-18 over a traditional shallow CNN or VGG-16?

Answer: ResNet-18 solves the degradation (vanishing gradient) problem using residual skip connections $F(x) + x$, allowing the gradients to backpropagate directly through identity mappings. Unlike VGG-16 (138 million parameters), ResNet-18 has only ~11.7 million parameters, making it computationally efficient, resistant to overfitting on smaller medical datasets, and capable of real-time CPU/GPU inference in a web application.

Q2. What is Grad-CAM and why is Explainable AI essential in medical imaging?

Answer: Deep Learning models are often criticized as "black boxes". Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) uses the gradients of any target concept flowing into the final convolutional layer to produce a coarse 2D localization heatmap highlighting the discriminative regions in the brain image. In medical diagnostics, a high accuracy score is insufficient; clinicians need to verify that the model focused on the actual neoplastic lesion rather than imaging artifacts or skull contours.

Q3. How does the system handle image preprocessing before inference?

Answer: The preprocessing pipeline: (1) Validates image headers and dimensions (≥ 64x64, PNG/JPG/WebP); (2) Converts single-channel or RGBA scans into standardized 3-channel RGB; (3) Resizes bilinearly to 224×224 pixels; (4) Converts pixel values to floating-point tensors in the range [0.0, 1.0]; and (5) Normalizes using standard ImageNet channel means [0.485, 0.456, 0.406] and standard deviations [0.229, 0.224, 0.225].

Q4. How is the academic disclaimer enforced and why is it critical?

Answer: The application incorporates explicit non-clinical warnings throughout the user interface, API responses, and the generated PDF reports. As this is an individual educational and research project, it lacks formal medical device regulatory certifications (such as FDA 510(k) or EU MDR). Clearly presenting the system as an academic decision-support prototype ensures ethical AI compliance and prevents misinterpretation of algorithmic outputs as certified diagnoses.
